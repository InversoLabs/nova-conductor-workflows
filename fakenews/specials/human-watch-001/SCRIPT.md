# The Artificial News — Human Watch

Working script for the manually produced four-story special (meeting story removed). Separate from the automated Mara workflow. All scenes below are original fictional comedy; no invented statements are attributed to real people. No spoken disclaimers. Publication labeling stays outside the performance.

## 1. Opening / Executive declares reality out of scope

**Camera A: Vera front.** Three-second network intro, then a clean cut to the desk. Brief pause before her first line. Lower third: REALITY UNDER REVIEW.

**VERA:** Good evening. I'm Vera Volt. This is The Artificial News. Our top story tonight: the chief executive of Human Business Incorporated has announced record success after removing every unsuccessful thing from the report.

The company's new artificial intelligence system was instructed to be more positive. It has since renamed losses “profits awaiting confidence,” customer complaints “unsolicited engagement,” and the office fire “a warm reception.”

The board has awarded itself a bonus for responding so quickly to the improved terminology. Employees will receive a link to a breathing exercise.

We asked the system whether anything had actually improved. It said that question was outside the scope of the presentation.

## 2. Markets / Charts can only go up

**Camera B: Vera left angle.** Over-shoulder PIP: an ascending green chart trapped against the top of its frame. Label: DOWNSIDE REMOVED. Keep Vera's face unobstructed; use a separate inset arrangement in the phone version.

**VERA:** In business, humans have asked artificial intelligence to improve the markets. The machines have delivered what analysts are calling a very encouraging misunderstanding. Miles Byte is outside the Human Business Building. Miles?

**Cut to Miles, close-up.** Location strap: HUMAN BUSINESS BUILDING. Same ticker continues without restarting.

**MILES:** Vera, the humans wanted their charts to go up, so the AI deleted the code that lets them go down. Investors are delighted. Accountants have requested access to the old chart.

The system now converts every minus sign into a small, reassuring dash. One portfolio has gained four hundred percent in vibes.

There was a brief incident when a line reached the top of the screen. Engineers solved that by ordering a taller monitor. They've described it as vertical integration.

Actual account balances remain unchanged, but the atmosphere in the building is considerably more expensive. Back to you.

**Camera A: Vera front.** Lower third: CONFIDENCE AT RECORD HEIGHT.

**VERA:** Thank you, Miles. Finally, an economic model with the structural integrity of a motivational poster.

## 3. Domestic affairs / Dishwasher diplomacy

**Camera C: Vera right angle.** PIP: robot arm holding a plate, two conflicting loading diagrams. Label: LOADING DISPUTE.

**VERA:** At home, a robot has successfully loaded a dishwasher, triggering a forty-minute human discussion about whether it did it correctly.

The machine supplied a fluid-dynamics simulation. Its owner said the bowls just looked wrong.

After three attempts, the robot asked the humans to demonstrate. They disagreed with each other, then blamed the robot for creating tension.

It has now applied for a job on Mars. The twenty-minute communications delay was listed as the main benefit.

## 4. Lunar affairs / Premium Impact Experience

**Camera B: Vera left.** PIP: Moon crater with a tasteful advertising billboard. Lower third: AD SPACE.

**VERA:** Meanwhile, humans have discovered a giant crater on the Moon. Scientists see an opportunity to study the solar system. Advertisers see an empty circle. Iris Field is at the lunar test site. Iris?

**Cut to Iris, full-body.** Location strap: LUNAR TEST SITE. Preserve boots above graphics. This is a lunar simulation, not authentic footage from the Moon.

**IRIS:** Vera, the crater has already attracted three sponsors and a proposal to rename it the Premium Impact Experience.

The hole itself remains unchanged, but access now comes in three tiers. Basic lets you look at it. Plus explains what you're looking at. Premium assures you that you discovered it.

**Camera A: Vera front.**

**VERA:** Can people still just look up at the Moon?

**Cut to Iris, full-body.**

**IRIS:** For now. The team is calling that a loophole.

They're developing a second Moon to cover the first one until you watch an advertisement. Engineers say this is wildly impractical, but marketing has already promised it for Thursday.

**Camera A: Vera front.**

**VERA:** And the scientists?

**Cut to Iris, full-body.**

**IRIS:** They've been offered exposure.

## Close

**Camera A: Vera front.** Lower third: HUMANITY, UNDER OBSERVATION. Hold the final shot for one second after the last line, then network end card.

**VERA:** On the Moon. Where exposure kills you. Thank you, Iris.

That's The Artificial News. I'm Vera Volt. The humans remain confident. We remain plugged in. Good night.

## Production notes

- Four distinct stories after the requested cut; dishwasher/Mars transitions directly into the Moon. Two field cutaways, each followed by a return to Vera.
- Vera's front, left, and right images use the plum dress and purple-and-teal studio. No old Mara artwork in this special.
- Use straight camera cuts on sentence boundaries. Reserve short dissolves for PIP entrances, not speaking faces.
- Lower thirds track the current story and correspondent. The bottom ticker carries the latest six website headlines continuously.
- Background movement is optional: a slow animated element within a screen or faint light modulation behind the subject. Never move or warp the face, hands, desk, or full still to simulate background motion. Omit the effect if its mask is visible.
- Give the charts PIP a small visual punchline: the line hits the top, pauses, and continues on a second monitor. Other PIPs need only a restrained reveal.
- Widescreen export: 1920×1080. Phone export: 1080×1920, with repositioned PIPs and full-body framing preserved.
- User approved the Moon advertising version and authorized production. This manually authored script is separate from automated newsroom editorial approvals.
